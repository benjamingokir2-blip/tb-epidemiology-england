# Tuberculosis in England: Epidemiological Analysis (2015–2024)

## Project Overview
A descriptive epidemiological analysis of tuberculosis (TB) notifications in England, examining national trends, regional variation and inequalities by area-level deprivation.

## Research Questions
1. How did TB notification counts and rates change between 2015 and 2024?
2. How did notification rates vary across UKHSA regions in 2024?
3. How did notification rates vary by deprivation decile between 2019 and 2024?

## Tools
- MySQL: data querying, aggregation and trend calculations
- Excel: data inspection and preparation
- Tableau Public: visualisation and dashboards

## Key Findings
- National TB notifications increased from 4,123 in 2020 to 5,490 in 2024.
- London recorded the highest regional notification rate in 2024 (20.6 per 100,000); the South West recorded the lowest (4.2).
- The notification-rate gap between the most and least deprived deciles increased from 11.6 per 100,000 in 2019 to 14.2 in 2024.
- In 2024, the rate in the most deprived decile was approximately 5.3 times that in the least deprived decile.

## Data Source
UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA), *Tuberculosis in England, 2025 report* and accompanying supplementary data.

https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/tuberculosis-in-england-2025-report

## Scope and Limitations
This is a descriptive analysis of reported TB notifications, not all TB infections. Regional analysis covers 2024, while deprivation analysis uses area-level deprivation deciles. Findings describe patterns and associations; they do not establish causation.
